Redesigning bank signup into a conversation that people in small-town Brazil could finish.

One question at a time, in plain words, for people who had never used a digital bank.

2021. 4 months. Voltz (Energisa group), through Blip Professional Services.

In Brazil, 31.6 million people live in towns of under 20,000, 69.5 million adults never finished high school, and 120 million are on WhatsApp.

Sources: IBGE population estimates, 2021. IBGE, PNAD Educação, 2019. WhatsApp’s own figure, 2017. About Energisa: energisa.com.br.

The company

  • Energisa is a Brazilian energy group with more than 120 years of history.
  • Its nine electricity distributors cover 24% of Brazil’s territory, and bring energy to hard-to-reach areas and poorer regions.

The product

  • Voltz is Energisa’s fintech.
  • Its digital account offers Pix, bill payments and a virtual card.
  • Customers get a discount when they pay their Energisa electricity bill in the app, plus cashback.

The problem

  • Most people didn’t finish the signup and activation. The old app asked for too many steps, and for many of them it was their first digital bank.
  • The client also saw a big opportunity: WhatsApp as a native channel to bring in new customers.

The proposed solution

  • A digital account redesign that turned the signup into a conversation in three stages, and left the heavy steps for later.
  • A simpler onboarding, to teach a digital bank to people who had never used one.

My direct contribution

  • Planned and led the Lean Inception workshop with key stakeholders from 5 areas, which produced the MVP and a new product vision.
  • Led the discovery together with the client: interviews, review analysis, benchmark and funnel analysis.
  • Redesigned the signup, activation and onboarding flow, using conversation design principles.
  • Set up an omnichannel strategy using WhatsApp.
  • Worked in pair with the client’s product team, as an external designer inside their squad.

The Lean Inception workshop.

Lean Inception is a workshop where people from different areas agree on what to build, for whom, and why. Here, 14 people from 5 areas worked out the product vision, the persona, the tone of voice, the features and the priorities. Below is a summary of the board.

1 · Product vision

For people in small towns and rural areas, whose bank signup is long and hard to finish, Voltz is a digital account that opens in a conversation. Unlike banks that ask for everything at once, it asks for less up front.

See it working.

  • The betA chat in simple words, asking for less up front, would get more people to the end.
  • Why a chatMany of these customers had never used a banking app, and a form puts a screen full of fields in front of them.
  • What we builtOne question per message, plain words, and errors that say what to do next.
  • Why three stagesThe account works after 10 steps. The document and the selfie wait until the person wants a higher limit.
Try it yourself
Stage 1 Account setup

This is the first contact, so it sets the tone. Nobody faces a screen full of fields. In six steps the person already has access to the app, and the SMS code fills itself in.

Stage 2 Activation

This stage turns access into an account that works. It only asks what opening the account needs, then releases the Pix. The person can stop after stage 1 and come back later, and nothing is lost.

Terms, inside the chat

Our bet was to rewrite the terms in everyday language, so people actually understand what they’re agreeing to. A link opens the full legal text.

Not everything stayed a conversation

An address is one place where people expect a form, so we used fields, as many apps do. Familiarity here means “I’ve filled this in before.” You type the ZIP code, and the street, city and state fill in.

Onboarding as conversation design: tone of voice to guide the first steps.

The pain

People just opened their first digital account.

Many have little experience with apps.

They don’t know where anything is.

The pattern

Tips on the real home, pointing at the real thing.

People have seen this before, so there’s nothing new to learn.

Any tip can be skipped.

The bet

Tone of voice and copy.

One plain sentence per feature, with the benefit first.

“Pague a fatura da Energisa e outras contas. Ganhe até 10% de crédito em novas faturas.”

Stage 3 Raise your limit

The account already works, with a lower limit. To raise it from R$1,000 to R$5,000, the person adds three things: their mother’s name, a photo of an ID and a selfie. In the old app these were the steps where most people gave up, so now they come later, as an invitation. Each step says how long it takes, and progress is kept.

InvitationThe higher limit first, then the three steps and how long each one takes.
CaptureThe ID photo and the selfie. Portrait only, with a frame that warns about glare before the photo is taken.
OutcomeApproved, or asked for a better photo, always with the reason. The home then shows the new limit.

The WhatsApp bet

WhatsApp brought people in. The app still had to finish the job.

WhatsApp is easy to reach in small towns, so it did bring in more signups. But the PIN and using the account always needed the app, and so did the proof of life and the document photo. We flagged this limit, and the client bet people would make the jump. Most finished the chat, then never downloaded the app.

Learning and takeaways

The conversation helped, up to a point.

The bet was that a chat in simple words, on the channel people already use, would get more of them to the end of the signup.

What worked

Most people who started the chat got to the end of it. WhatsApp also brought in more potential customers, through Energisa’s own campaigns.

Where it broke

The chat ended with a link to download the app. Our persona is at ease on WhatsApp, but finds an app hard to download and feels unsure about new technology, especially people over 50. To me, the link read as a break in expectation: the chat had felt easy, and then it asked for something hard.

What I took from it

To me, a handoff between channels is part of the experience. Here it was a link, and for this audience it was the weakest step.

The project had a closed scope. When the contract ended, the client chose not to continue.

Where it is today

The Voltz app no longer exists. The group later moved its digital services into Energisa On, to stay close to the Energisa brand and the electricity bill people already know. Voltz is still a company, and much of the conversation design and tone of voice carried over.
